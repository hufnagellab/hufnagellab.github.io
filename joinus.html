RESEARCH STAFF New York, NY · Mount Sinai

Associate Researcher I — Neuroscience

The Hufnagel Lab at the Icahn School of Medicine at Mount Sinai investigates the molecular, cellular, and neural mechanisms underlying individual vulnerability to alcohol use disorder. We are seeking a research-oriented individual interested in contributing to experimental studies of alcohol drinking behavior and compulsive-like alcohol use in mouse models.

Research Responsibilities

  • Conduct mouse behavioral experiments, including alcohol self-administration and drinking paradigms.
  • Assist with mouse colony management, animal handling, and experimental monitoring.
  • Perform or assist with stereotaxic surgeries and experimental procedures.
  • Collect brain tissue for molecular and cellular analyses.
  • Maintain accurate experimental records and organized datasets.
  • Contribute to data analysis, scientific presentations, and manuscript preparation.

Qualifications

  • Bachelor's or Master's degree in neuroscience, biology, or a related scientific discipline.
  • Previous laboratory research experience is preferred.
  • Experience handling laboratory mice and conducting behavioral experiments is highly desirable.
  • Familiarity with rodent behavioral paradigms or alcohol self-administration is advantageous.
  • Strong organizational skills, attention to detail, and scientific curiosity.
  • Ability to work collaboratively in an interdisciplinary research environment.

02 / OUR CULTURE

Life in the Lab

The Hufnagel Lab brings together experimental neuroscience and computational biology to investigate fundamental questions about individual differences in behavior and susceptibility to psychiatric disorders.

Our research spans behavioral neuroscience, molecular and cellular biology, transcriptomics, epigenomics, and functional approaches. We believe that meaningful discoveries emerge when scientists with complementary expertise challenge assumptions and explore problems from different perspectives.

We are committed to creating an environment where trainees develop scientific independence, technical expertise, critical thinking, and the confidence to pursue ambitious ideas.
